# E-Commerce Customer Segmentation using RFM and Clustering

## Business Problem
The e-commerce landscape is highly competitive, and treating all customers with a generalized marketing approach leads to inefficient ad spending and lower retention rates. The goal of this project is to use data-driven decision-making to transition from a "one-size-fits-all" marketing strategy to a targeted, personalized approach. By segmenting the customer base, the company aims to identify high-value customer groups, improve customer retention through tailored re-engagement, and optimize marketing efficiency.

## Dataset Description
The dataset contains transaction-level e-commerce data. 
* **Rows:** Each row represents a single line-item purchase within a larger invoice.
* **Key Columns:** `InvoiceNo`, `StockCode`, `Description`, `Quantity`, `InvoiceDate`, `UnitPrice`, `CustomerID`, and `Country`.
* **Nature of Data:** International retail dataset containing transactional history, encompassing metrics required to analyze purchasing behavior over time.

## Data Cleaning Summary
To ensure the integrity of the clustering model, the following data cleaning steps were performed:
1. **Missing Values:** Dropped records with missing `CustomerID` as segmentation cannot be performed without user identification.
2. **Anomalies & Returns:** Removed transactions with negative or zero `Quantity` (typically representing returned or cancelled orders) and zero `UnitPrice` (system errors or free gifts).
3. **Duplicates:** Identified and removed exact duplicate rows to prevent skewed frequency and monetary calculations.
4. **Data Types:** Converted `InvoiceDate` to a datetime object for accurate Recency calculations and cast `CustomerID` to a string.

## Feature Engineering Summary
The data was transformed from transaction-level to customer-level using the RFM framework:
* **Recency (R):** Number of days since the customer's most recent purchase, calculated against a baseline snapshot date (1 day post the maximum date in the dataset).
* **Frequency (F):** The total number of unique invoices (transactions) generated by the customer.
* **Monetary (M):** The total revenue generated by the customer (`Quantity` * `UnitPrice` summed across all their transactions).
* *Additional Features:* Total quantity purchased and the primary country of the customer were also aggregated.

* **Revenue Distribution:** The dataset is heavily right-skewed, indicating a small percentage of customers generate the majority of the revenue.
* **Geographical Dominance:** [Insert Top Country, e.g., The United Kingdom] dominates the total transaction volume and revenue generation.
* **Product Performance:** A small subset of unique `StockCodes` drives the highest sales volume, suggesting a Pareto principle (80/20 rule) in product popularity.

## Clustering Approach
1. **Transformation:** Due to the severe right-skewness of the RFM variables, a Log Transformation (`np.log1p`) was applied to normalize the distributions.
2. **Scaling:** Data was standardized using `StandardScaler` to ensure the `Monetary` value did not heavily outweigh `Recency` and `Frequency` during distance calculations.
3. **Algorithm:** K-Means clustering was deployed. The Elbow Method was utilized to determine the optimal number of clusters, resulting in `k=3` distinct customer segments.

## Cluster Interpretation
Based on the unscaled RFM averages, the customer base was segmented into three distinct personas:
* **Cluster 0 (The Champions):** High Frequency, High Monetary, Low Recency. These are the most valuable and loyal customers who purchase often and spend heavily.
* **Cluster 1 (At-Risk / Inactive):** Low Frequency, Low Monetary, High Recency. These customers made a small purchase long ago and have not returned.
* **Cluster 2 (Recent / Occasional Buyers):** Low Frequency, Mid Monetary, Low Recency. These are newer customers who have interacted recently but have not yet established a habitual purchasing pattern.

## Final Business Recommendations
1. **For Champions (Cluster 0):** Implement an exclusive VIP loyalty tier. Do not offer standard discount codes, as these users are largely price-insensitive. Instead, offer early access to new product drops and dedicated customer support to maintain exclusivity.
2. **For At-Risk Customers (Cluster 1):** Deploy an automated "win-back" email sequence. Offer a highly aggressive, time-bound personalized discount (e.g., "25% off your entire cart for the next 48 hours") to reactivate their purchasing behavior before they churn completely.
3. **For Recent Buyers (Cluster 2):** Focus on increasing order frequency and Average Order Value (AOV). Implement targeted cross-selling campaigns based on their initial purchase category (e.g., if they bought electronics, recommend relevant accessories) with a "Buy One, Get One 10% off" incentive.
